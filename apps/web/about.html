How to play

Tessera is a strategy game about territory. Every empire starts from its capital somewhere on a big grid, and every tile is up for grabs. Push out into empty land, attack where you're strong, and outflank a neighbour before they outflank you. When the time runs out, whoever holds the most ground wins.

Play Tessera

Free, in a web browser, on a computer, tablet or phone. Play on your own against bots, or online with other people in games that keep running while everybody is away, so a game can last for days.

Claiming ground

  • Your bank fills over time, up to 999 (the meter at the top). Click a tile on the edge of your land and you spend the whole bank on it.
  • An empty tile becomes yours, holding the population you spent. An enemy tile loses what you spent: take it below zero and it's yours.
  • Click a tile you already own to thicken it, so it's harder to take.
  • A tile your land surrounds on more sides takes more: up to four times the bank when it's boxed in on all four.
  • So the choice is always the same: spend little and often to spread, or wait for a full bank and hit hard.

The map

  • Mountains and lakes can't be crossed. Rivers and walls can, once somebody bridges or ladders them, and then they're open to everyone.
  • Land cut off from your capital slowly withers, so keep your territory joined up.
  • Hover over a tile (with a mouse) to see what it is and what a click there would do.

Coins and diamonds

  • Coins (bronze, silver and gold) appear around the map, often just outside somebody's border. Claim one and it sets off a cascade: the population spreads across the tiles around it. Gold spreads furthest. Cascades are the fastest way to gain ground.
  • Diamonds are what the store takes. Claim the tile one sits on to pick it up.

The store

  • Bridge B and Ladder L: cross a river or a wall. Once you hold one, the tiles where it can go light up; click one to place it.
  • March M: from then on, your claims can reach two tiles out instead of one.
  • Growth G: from then on, land joined to your capital gains population by itself.

Capitals

  • Your capital is your home. Lose it and your empire is out, and whoever takes it takes your land with it.
  • A new empire's capital is protected for its first few minutes, or until it grows, so nobody is knocked out before they've started.

Playing online

  • New game makes a game and gives you a link to send. Each empire can have several players sharing it, each with their own bank.
  • People can join for the first part of a game: take an empire nobody holds, ask an empire's players to let them in, or use an invite link from one of them.
  • The game keeps running when you close the tab. Come back from Your games on the start page and you're straight back in.
  • An empire whose players have all been away for a long time counts as abandoned, so a long game won't wait forever for somebody who has left. Being asleep for a night is fine.
  • When a game ends, it's kept so anyone can watch it back from Finished.

Where it comes from

Tessera is a recreation of Empire Attack, the online grid game that ran from 2008 to 2017. It was conceived by Ian Andrew and designed jointly with Jon Grove, who programmed it. You can read about the original on Ian Andrew's Empire Attack page.

Tessera is an independent recreation and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by its creators. It's made in New Zealand, and its source is on GitHub.
